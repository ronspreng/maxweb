Supplements for Brain Health: What Works and What Doesn't

Published: May 2026
Updated: May 10, 2026
Read Time: 6 minutes
Category: Brain Health & Wellness
Evidence-Based Research Expert Reviewed Medically Accurate Updated 2026

Not all supplements are created equal. Some have strong scientific support for cognitive benefits, while others lack evidence. Here's what the research actually shows.

Evidence-Supported Supplements

Omega-3 Fatty Acids (Fish Oil)

Strong evidence for supporting brain cell structure and reducing neuroinflammation. Typical dose: 1,000-2,000mg EPA+DHA daily.

B Vitamins

B6, B12, and folate are essential for neurotransmitter production. Deficiency impairs cognition.

Magnesium

Supports neural plasticity and memory formation. Many people are deficient.

Antioxidants

Vitamins C and E, CoQ10, and anthocyanins protect brain cells from oxidative damage.

Promising but Emerging

  • Phosphatidylserine: Shows promise for memory
  • Alpha-GPC: May support cognitive decline prevention
  • Bacopa Monnieri: Traditional herb with some cognitive support evidence

Insufficient or Lacking Evidence

Be cautious with supplements making dramatic cognitive claims without substantial research support.

Important Considerations

  • Supplements complement, not replace, lifestyle factors
  • Quality varies—use reputable brands third-party tested
  • Consult healthcare providers before starting
  • Effects are subtle and accumulate over time
  • Whole foods provide better nutrient bioavailability

The Bottom Line

Before spending money on supplements, optimize the basics: sleep, nutrition, exercise, and stress management. These provide 80% of cognitive benefits. Supplements are the additional 20%.
